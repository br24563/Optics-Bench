How the physics works

Ideal lenses use the classic thin-lens graphical construction: a ray through the optical center is never bent, so every ray parallel to it must refract to cross the same point on the focal plane.

Realistic lenses trace actual Snell's law through two spherical glass surfaces, using each surface's radius of curvature and the glass's refractive index — including real spherical aberration (rays far from the axis focus slightly differently than paraxial rays, just like a real lens).

Mirrors reflect with angle of incidence equal to angle of reflection; curved mirrors add a focusing correction equivalent to a thin lens of focal length R/2.

The prism refracts through two real flat glass faces. Its refractive index depends on wavelength (Cauchy's equation), so shorter (bluer) wavelengths bend more — the same reason prisms split white light into a rainbow.
